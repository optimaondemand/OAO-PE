What Counts as Physical Activity?

Physical activity is any movement that makes your body work and use energy. That covers a lot of ground — way more than just sports. Running, climbing, dancing, biking, hiking, swimming, even raking leaves all count.

Here's something important: PE isn't only for kids who love sports or want to be professional athletes. Every body needs movement to stay healthy and strong. Doctors, scientists, teachers, video game designers — everyone needs to move their body almost every day. Your goal isn't to be the fastest or the strongest. It's to keep moving in ways that feel good to you.

Did you know?

Your heart beats around 100,000 times every day — even while you sleep. During hard exercise, it can speed up to nearly double that rate to push extra oxygen out to your working muscles. Over a lifetime, that's about 2.5 billion heartbeats. Treating your heart well now pays off for decades.

Movement Changes How You Feel

Physical activity doesn't just change your body — it also changes your mood and how your brain works. When you exercise, your brain releases chemicals called endorphins — natural mood boosters that can leave you feeling calmer and happier for hours after a workout.

Real-world connection

Professional athletes like Serena Williams, LeBron James, and Simone Biles all talk about how movement helps their mental game just as much as their physical performance. Even on rest days, they walk, stretch, or do light yoga — because they know movement keeps their minds sharp and their stress low.

PE Builds More Than Muscles

Physical education classrooms (and playgrounds) are also where you build sportsmanship — the habit of being respectful, fair, and supportive whether you're winning or losing. When you play games with classmates, you practice:

  • Teamwork — figuring out how to coordinate with people who have different strengths.
  • Following rules — making sure the game stays fair for everyone.
  • Encouraging others — cheering for a teammate who's struggling or congratulating someone who beat you.
  • Handling losses — staying positive when things don't go your way.

These skills carry over into everything — group projects, family life, future jobs.
