Objective 1
Why Physical Activity Matters

Physical activity is any movement that gets your heart pumping and your muscles working. It includes obvious things like sports and PE, but also walking the dog, raking leaves, riding bikes, or dancing in your room. Health experts recommend kids your age get at least 60 minutes of activity every day — and not all in one stretch.

Why so much? Because 5th grade is the start of a huge growth window. Your bones are getting denser, your heart is getting bigger, and the wiring between your brain and muscles is being built. Movement is the signal that tells your body to build that hardware well.

🧪Sport Science

Your heart beats about 100,000 times every day, pumping roughly 2,000 gallons of blood. During hard exercise, your heart rate can climb from about 70 beats per minute (resting) to 180 or more. The blood it sends out carries oxygen to your muscles. The more efficient that delivery system gets, the longer you can keep moving without slowing down. That efficiency is called your aerobic capacity.

How Movement Helps Your Brain

Exercise isn't just a body thing. When you move, your brain releases chemicals called endorphins that lift your mood and help you handle stress. Physical activity also boosts BDNF — a protein that helps brain cells grow and connect.

💡Did You Know?

Brain scans show that students who exercise before school have more activity in their prefrontal cortex — the part of the brain you use for paying attention, solving problems, and making good decisions. That's why a quick movement break can help when you're stuck on hard math.

Movement Builds Teammates and Leaders

PE also trains skills you can't measure with a stopwatch: how to communicate, how to lose without quitting, how to lift teammates up. These are sometimes called character skills, and athletes work on them just like they work on speed and strength.
